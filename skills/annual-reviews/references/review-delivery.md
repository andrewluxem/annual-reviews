# Annual review delivery and calibration

Read this reference when the user needs to prepare the review conversation, handle peer input, or test rating consistency.

## Review conversation order

1. **State the purpose and scope.** Name the review period and what the conversation will cover.
2. **Invite the employee's summary.** Ask what they consider most important before presenting the manager draft.
3. **Discuss contributions and strengths.** Use specific evidence and supported impact.
4. **Discuss growth priorities.** Address one priority at a time and separate observed behavior from interpretation.
5. **Discuss a human-supplied rating, if applicable.** Use the supplied rubric and acknowledge missing or contrary evidence. The skill does not recommend or populate the rating.
6. **Agree on goals and support.** Give each action an owner, due date, measure, and review date.
7. **Capture the employee's response.** Record context, disagreement, or additional evidence without arguing it away in the meeting.
8. **Close with next steps.** Restate decisions, owners, dates, and any question requiring a separate authorized process.

Do not surprise the employee with an unsupported allegation. If evidence is new, incomplete, or disputed, label that state and follow the organization's process rather than forcing closure in the draft.

## Handle feedback from different directions

- **Self-reflection:** treat it as the employee's perspective and compare it with supplied work evidence.
- **Peer feedback:** preserve specific observed contributions while protecting any promised anonymity.
- **Upward feedback:** separate leadership behavior from personal preference and preserve the stated confidentiality boundary.
- **Manager feedback:** connect statements to role expectations, values, goals, and observed impact.

Do not present the number of reviewers, repeated wording, or contextual clues if doing so could identify an anonymous source.

## Calibration questions

Use these questions to inspect consistency in a rating supplied by an authorized human, not to recommend a rating or rank people automatically:

- Which supplied evidence supports this rating criterion?
- What evidence from another part of the review period could change the conclusion?
- Is a shared outcome being credited or blamed accurately?
- Are style and visibility being confused with contribution?
- Does the same behavior receive the same interpretation across comparable roles?
- Is the growth area limiting results, other people, or a stated expectation?
- How did the person handle ambiguity, adversity, or a stretch assignment, based on supplied evidence?
- What support or opportunity would test the next development hypothesis?

## Delivery record

After the conversation, capture:

| Item | Owner | Due date | Evidence of completion |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Review correction, evidence follow-up, goal, or support action] | [Owner needed] | [Date needed] | [Artifact or result] |

Keep compensation, promotion, termination, and other formal decisions in their authorized process. The review record may reference a separate decision, but it should not invent or make one.
